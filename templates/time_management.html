Which study session should I choose?

Compare work-and-break rhythms to find a pace that fits your task.

Show guide

50 Minutes Study / 10 Minutes Break

Best for: Deep focus, essay writing, and complex problem-solving.

Why it works: A focused block of about 45–50 minutes gives you time to settle into demanding work. A 10-minute break offers a chance to reset before the next block.

25 Minutes Study / 5 Minutes Break

Best for: Beating procrastination, quick review sessions, and heavy memorization.

Why it works: Short work intervals can make it easier to get started and sustain attention. After four sessions, take a longer 15–30 minute break.

15 Minutes Study / 3 Minutes Break

Best for: Flashcards, quick review, or starting a task that feels overwhelming.

Why it works: A short, clearly bounded session lowers the barrier to beginning and makes review easy to fit between other activities.

45 Minutes Study / 15 Minutes Break

Best for: Homework sessions, textbook reading, and a class-like study block.

Why it works: A moderate work period leaves enough time to make progress, while a full 15-minute pause creates room to move, hydrate, or reset.

52 Minutes Study / 17 Minutes Break

Best for: A structured focus block when you have a longer study window.

Why it works: This work-and-rest ratio is inspired by a Draugiem Group workplace productivity study. Treat it as one option to try; the best rhythm can vary from person to person.

90 Minutes Study / 20 Minutes Break

Best for: Advanced deep-work blocks when you are rested and already comfortable sustaining focus.

Why it works: Some people prefer a longer uninterrupted block followed by a substantial pause. If your focus fades sooner, choose a shorter preset instead.
